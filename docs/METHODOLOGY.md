# Analytics Methodology

This document explains, in plain language, how each analysis is calculated —
so you can judge whether it's appropriate for your decision, not just trust it.

## KPIs (Business Performance Dashboard)

Standard sums/ratios over the mapped, filtered rows: revenue = sum of the
revenue column; AOV = revenue ÷ distinct order count; profit margin = profit ÷
revenue, etc. Every metric is only shown when its required column(s) are
present — a missing column produces an explanation, never a fabricated zero.

## Data Quality score

A 0–100 score computed by deducting a weighted penalty per issue type
(critical/high/medium/low), scaled by what share of your rows each issue
affects. It is a heuristic summary for triage, not a certification.

## Customer segmentation (RFM)

Recency (days since last order), Frequency (order count), and Monetary
(lifetime revenue) per customer, mapped to labels (New, Repeat, Loyal, High
Value, At Risk, Inactive) using thresholds you can adjust in the Segmentation
view. Defaults: 4+ orders = Loyal, $500+ lifetime = High Value, 90+ days since
last order = At Risk, 180+ days = Inactive. **This is a configurable heuristic
lens, not an objective classification** — two businesses with identical data
might reasonably choose different thresholds.

## Sentiment analysis (in-browser)

A transparent lexicon-based estimator: review text is tokenized, matched
against a small list of positive/negative words, with simple negation
handling ("not good" flips the polarity of "good"). This is **an estimate**,
not a trained machine-learning model and not a clinical instrument. It will
miss sarcasm, idioms, and domain-specific language. The optional Python
Analyst Toolkit includes a real trained Naive Bayes classifier with proper
evaluation metrics for buyers who want a more rigorous (but still
demo-scale) approach.

## Forecasting

A simple linear-trend extrapolation: a straight line is fit to recent
history and projected forward. The uncertainty band widens the further into
the future the forecast goes, reflecting genuinely declining confidence — it
is not a decorative flourish. This method does **not** model seasonality,
promotions, or external events, requires at least 14 historical data points,
and is explicitly not a guarantee of future results.

## Anomaly detection

Flags points that deviate from a rolling mean by more than a configurable
number of standard deviations (default 2) within a rolling window (default 7
periods). A flat baseline with a single deviating point is also flagged, since
any deviation from a genuinely constant history is meaningful.

## Data Story Engine

Every sentence is generated by directly reading already-computed metrics —
there is no external AI call and no free-text generation. Each insight
includes a "Show Evidence" panel listing the exact figures the sentence is
built from, so every claim is independently checkable against the dashboard.

## Scenario Planner

Pure arithmetic on assumptions you enter (target revenue, AOV, price/discount
change, etc.) — never a prediction. Results are visually separated into
"historical data," "your assumptions," and "calculated scenario" so the three
are never confused.
